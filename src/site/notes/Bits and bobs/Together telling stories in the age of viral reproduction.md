**

February 2022 

  
  

Stories are not viruses. Stories travel faster.  That is terrifying.  We link together our constellations of data, titbits, odds and ends, scavenged from culture, memory, 3 am doom scrolling, family wisdom and our own sense of being in the world and it becomes a story just as soon as we share it.   

  

We have been here together telling stories for as long as there has been a we to tell. Since language mutated from pointing at what is - to pointing at what could be. Language like our shared DNA contains the wreckage of all human stories. The story passed from mouth to ear that ties us together and tears us apart.

  

Stories and how they are told are not just political; they are politics. The story we tell about the boot on the neck or the hand on the arm. Reflecting the current moment our stories are mostly told by individuals who have a single copy right. I own this piece because I wrote it. I am the hero of this story, the main character to be loved, adored or hated and materially rewarded. Individualist stories about unique individuals.  

  

 Stories still jump from eye to ear and mouth to mouth as we learn to think in space together. Folk still tell tales even while Marvel™ has become its own genre. We have always told stories together, always influenced and informed and reworked. Material conditions and the myth of the solitary or self made genius/creator/entrepreneur/maker etc. mean that there must be one name on the top billing but in many places communal narratives are being shaped.

  

In writers rooms across Tv land, in forum posts and copypasta, amazing living memes and horrendous communal conspiracy theories,  global networks of researchers and scientists writing and thinking together, folk songs and folks stories which rarely have a single author,  writers  in assemblages inspired by Deleuze and Guattari, in your local third spaces, at the family table, novels written by groups, the daring work and social histories of Svetlana Alexievich, the lines between  us and them breakdown in and through these projects. The method proposed here is simple to sit down with a group of people from different backgrounds and make a story  together.  To enter into the fictive dream, the cycle of writing and rewriting together.  

  

Inspired by community & socially engaged art practitioners (Kate O’Shea, Fiona Whelan, Broken Talkers, Juan Delgado, Francois Matteroi and many more)   I intend to learn from people who have done creative & difficult things before me. 

  

I will use a needs based approach which means that I will ensure that material and emotional needs for myself and collaborators are met first and foremost. This means people are paid adequately and equally for equal work. That the accessibility needs of all involved are met. That the work is done with empathy and care for the people involved and the stories they carry and those we shape together. 

  

I will be transparent in all my dealings. I will inform those who agree to collaborate with me what the project is and what their commitment is, what payment is, build in the ability to walk away, work towards as horizontal a collaboration as possible while acknowledging systemic and inherited inequalities and biases.  Everything will be negotiated and agreed based on informed consent.

  

We will share authorship. This will be a work written by more than one person and credited as such. 

  

I will not dictate the shape of the project except to say we are telling a story together and a shared text will form an element of that project. The outcome is unknown. We will start open to all possibilities and narrow as we collectively make decisions. 

  

We will aim for consensus on every decision. 

  

The exact shape the project form will differ but suffice to say will talk, write and plan together. Use recordings, books and movies and whatever else to collage and montage a story together knowing that all stories are woven this way just often in one person's head. Our writings may be shingled or written over or be stories told in relay. 

  

I acknowledge my perspective as a white western male presenting person, I am not an authority on other people's lives or an expert in all fields, I am not doing this to disavow my ego or assuage my guilt but because I think there is power in this method to tell stories with more nuance and perhaps even form stories that would otherwise not be told. I believe collaborations of these sorts begin the work of building a new world inside the shell of the old. 

  

I acknowledge the messiness of language, of stories and of collaborations. I have no interest in moral purity and inaction. I know that capitalism appropriates everything but we are not yet at the end of history. There are more stories to be told. Knowing the trauma and the horror and the biases we inherit from the world, what happens when we choose to work together to tell complicated stories?
