Many years ago in college I took up a philosophy module called anarchy law and the state. What did this course talk about? Democratic structures or the history of anarchist thought? Nope it was mostly about a yank called Rothbard and his distinctly American form of libertarianism, with a lecturer who was a very Catholic conservative. This was a trap for young radicals. I had not formed my own definition of anarchism or a political philosophy beyond looking at the capatlist nightmare and saying this ain't it. I knew immediately this "anarchy" was not it.

So, let's sketch out what libertarianism is and it's problems and in doing that we will come to a better understanding of what I understand anarchist thought to be.
Firstly a bit of the Greek. Anarchy means without a leader. An - without and archy from archon meaning leader from archos which in turn means power. Wikipedia tells me reliably. (An anarchist encyclopedia.)

As I sat in that class I leaned about a particular brand of libertarianism. If you have a different definition and don't want this one imposed on you I would expect nothing lesd but from here I speak from my POV on this version of libertarianism.

So, libertarianism is the belief that a central governing authority of any kind should not exist. Which makes it a kind of anarchism by definition. The free market remains though and individuals are thought to act in natural productive self interest.  Taxation under this world view in any form is theft on par with organ harvesting. Private property is sacrosanct the organising principal is ownership. Those and in this new state of warring free enterprises would be governed by contract law alone. Agreements between individuals or business entities, a consent based system mediates all rights but that of private property.

This brief paragraph though revealing my own bias against this worldview paints a pretty good picture of libertarianism as Rothbard descibes it. It is the ideology of billionaires and all those looking to return to the frontier or the imagined frontier of the American west. 

This is often what anarchism is imagined as a effective return to the state of nature. Or an all out no holds battle royal. This is not what anarchism means to me. To me it is a system of cooperative decision making that aims to address the issue of permanent hierarchy. Unlike libertarianism that assumes hierarchy is natural, inevitable and good, other forms of anarchist thought propose consensus based decision making and organising hierarchies that are temporary or can be changed. 

What does libertarianism look like in practice? In real terms the behaviour of many large multinational companies. They act with impunity and only held back by contracts. The recent leveling of a Finnish forest by Meta is one such example.
How does non hierarchical anarchy function in real life? A healthy friendship group is an easy familiar example. No one is in charge. From time to time someone takes up the mantle of organising. Resources are shared. Boundaries respected. 

People need leaders though the Greek chorus cries after being awakened from sleep by earlier mentions. I think we are really socially conditioned in a deep and significant sense to believe this but anyone that has worked for any large organisation would agree that most if not all of the day to day work would go on without leaders. We need organisation we need plans and structures. Leaders more often thab not present a vision or organising philosophy which they implement with a core group of insiders or higher ups. We can think this shit up ourselves. You know write a constitution or a way of governing ourselves do a bit of democracy. The argument often is that this doesn't work at scale. I would counter what about the Zapatista's or Rojiva or the Paris commune or the huge workers cooperative in Spain and across Europe. We have representative democracy and that is anarchism for billionaires. I think we should have anarchism for ordinary people in their communities and work places. That for me means freedom. The ability to make decisions about your life. The way you do that is by speaking to your neighbours. We live in a very narrow parameters of social organisation. We could do otherwise. What would the world look like if freedom and human happiness and ecological valance was the organising principal rather than profit and power?

I'll come back to this and dig up some anarchist theorists I like. I write this to remind myself of that class all those years ago and to begin to dig deep into what anarchism means to me now. This will be series exploring my life as a community organiser and educator in Ireland and how those struggles have shaped this thing with feathers that sings still in my heart. A hope for freedom. I call it anarchism, what do you call it?
